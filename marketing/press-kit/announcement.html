NumPad 2.0: the keyboard that finishes the math before you hit =

More Voltage · July 2026 · App Store

NumPad has done one job since 2016: put a real numeric keypad — the kind you'd find on a calculator or a cash register — inside every app on your iPhone. Banking apps, spreadsheets, group chats where someone has to split the bill. It turns out a lot of typing is just numbers, and phone keyboards have always treated numbers as an afterthought.

2.0 is the biggest release in the app's history, and it starts from a simple observation: when someone types 84.50*1.18 into a text field, they don't want the expression. They want the answer.

Math that runs ahead of you

So now the keyboard computes while you type. Start an expression and a small chip floats up with the running result — finish typing 84.50*1.18 and it's already showing 99.71. Tap the chip and the answer lands in the field. No app-switch to Calculator, no copy-paste round trip. It's the same engine that powers the keyboard's = key, so the two can never disagree.

That engine now also answers to Siri. "Calculate with NumPad," "Convert units with NumPad," and "Calculate a tip with NumPad" work from Siri and Spotlight the moment the app is installed — zero configuration, and entirely offline. A number you compute by voice even shows up in the keyboard's result history.

The rest of 2.0

Two new key packs — Units & Conversion and Cooking & Baking — join Finance, Symbols & Science, Programmer, and Date & Time, and both come with a live converter a long-press away. Pro users get a Custom Keyboard editor: drag keys from a library, watch a live preview, flip the whole layout for left-handed use. On iOS 26 there's a theme pair rendering Apple's real Liquid Glass material. And the iPad finally gets treated like an iPad: overlays open beside your work instead of on top of it, keys respond to pointer hover, and a new Kiosk height preset makes the keypad legible from arm's length — built for iPads that live at a front desk or a checkout counter rather than in a pocket.

The part I'm stubborn about

NumPad costs $2.99, the packs are $1.99 each, and Pro is $11.99 — once. There is no subscription anywhere in the app, and every purchase is Family Sharing enabled. The keyboard extension ships with no analytics SDK at all: it's not that we promise not to look at your keystrokes, it's that there's nothing in the binary that could. A keyboard is the most intimate software on a phone. It should act like it.
