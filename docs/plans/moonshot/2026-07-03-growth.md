Platform facts checked via WebSearch (not assumed from training data, since load-bearing):
- **Apple Offer Codes now cover consumable, non-consumable, and non-renewing IAPs — not just
  subscriptions** (2025 App Store Connect update). This is new enough that most utility/keyboard
  apps haven't built around it yet. Promo Codes (the old app-level giveaway mechanism) are being
  retired for IAPs starting March 26, 2026 — Offer Codes are the forward-compatible path.
  [Apple: Create offer codes for In-App Purchases](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-in-app-purchases/create-offer-codes-for-in-app-purchases/),
  [9to5Mac: promo codes phase-out 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2025/10/29/apple-adds-new-app-store-submission-and-marketing-features-will-phase-out-promo-codes-in-2026/)
- **Apple Business Manager Custom Apps** support org-scoped distribution and a **50%-off
  education-quantity-20+ pricing lever** set at submission time; no code changes needed to make an
  existing binary a B2B SKU. [Apple: Volume Purchase and Custom Apps](https://developer.apple.com/support/volume-purchase-and-custom-apps/)
- **KeyboardKit Pro** (closest direct comp for a keyboard SDK) sells via tiered
  indie/business/custom licenses, confirming the B2B brief's $50–500/mo precedent is realistic, not
  invented. [KeyboardKit pricing](https://keyboardkit.com/pricing), [Business Licenses](https://keyboardkit.com/business)

Constraints applied to every idea below: keyboard extension ≤50MB, no network from the extension
without Full Access, no Firebase in the extension, iOS 16 floor (iOS 26+ gated behind
`#available`), buildable incrementally by a small team, nothing that's a one-week gimmick.

## 1. Offer Code Referral Loop

**Pitch.** Apple's 2025 expansion of Offer Codes to non-consumables means NumPad can now let a
happy customer *gift* a real pack to a friend using Apple's own redemption rails — no backend, no
custom licensing server, no gray-market code reseller risk. Add a "Gift a Pack" row to the Store
screen that shares an ASC-hosted offer-code redemption link (Messages/AirDrop/social) for the
cheapest pack; the recipient taps it, StoreKit's native redemption sheet opens, and they get the
pack free, permanently, tied to *their* Apple ID. Layer this on top of the Family Sharing that's
already ON for all 8 non-consumables (per CLAUDE.md) and NumPad has two distinct viral loops for
the price of zero new StoreKit plumbing: "share with someone outside your family" (offer code) and
"everyone in your household already has this" (Family Sharing, already shipped but never marketed
as a feature). The existing deep-link attribution pattern (`numpad://store-preview?source=...` →
`store_viewed` logging in `StoreViewController`) extends trivially to a `source=gift_redeemed` tag.
**No subscription, no server, no new UIKit surface bigger than one Store row** — it's almost
entirely App Store Connect configuration plus a share sheet.

**Wow moment.** A customer becomes an acquisition channel with one tap, using an Apple mechanic
(non-consumable Offer Codes) that shipped in 2025 and almost no competing keyboard app has
discovered yet.

**Effort:** M — mostly ASC campaign setup + a Store-screen share row + `AppStore
.presentCodeRedemptionSheet()`/URL-based redemption; the hard StoreKit work (purchase/restore
listeners) already exists in `StoreManager`.

**Risk.** Offer-code pools are a manually replenished, finite ASC resource (not a self-serve
infinite mint) — needs a light ops cadence to keep the pool stocked and to detect leaked/public
codes being farmed outside the intended one-friend-at-a-time flow. Framing must stay "gift," not
"referral reward," to avoid any pay-for-install App Review ambiguity (gifting is explicitly
supported; incentivized referral schemes draw more scrutiny).

**First shippable slice.** Stand up ONE offer-code campaign against the cheapest existing pack
(Date & Time, $1.99) with a small hand-generated code pool (~500), add a single "Gift this pack"
button in the Store screen that opens Messages with the ASC redemption link, and tag the resulting
`store_viewed`/purchase events with `source=gift` using the attribution pattern that already ships.

## 2. Kiosk Fleet License (B2B, zero new code)

**Pitch.** The B2B brief already concludes Product A (the keyboard extension, deployed and
locked down via SAM/ASAM/Guided Access) is the right fit for the *low-volume, don't-own-the-app,
one-time-budget* segment of inbound interest — and that segment is exactly what "kiosk data-entry
station" inbound leads look like. Don't wait for the undesigned Product B SDK: package **today's
binary** as an Apple Business Manager **Custom App**, visible only to the requesting
organization's Apple Business Manager ID, sold at a business-negotiated volume price (Apple's own
Custom App program supports 50%-off pricing at 20+ education-quantity submission — the general
custom-app volume-pricing lever is the same mechanism, just negotiated per org). Pair it with a
paid "Deployment Kit" service tier — the enable-keyboard/Full-Access/SAM lock-down playbook already
written up in `docs/kiosk/NumPad-for-Kiosks.md` — turned into a one-time setup fee. This is a
**services-plus-existing-binary** business line, not a new product to engineer.

**Wow moment.** The exact app that ships to consumers today becomes a recurring B2B revenue line
through Apple's own Custom App / volume-purchase rails, with zero new Swift code.

**Effort:** S — new ASC Custom App record, an org-scoped price, and sales/support collateral; at
most a tiny Remote-Config-gated welcome screen for the B2B build, reusing `RemoteConfigManager`.

**Risk.** Apple's Custom App program is not self-serve — the *organization* has to be the one
reaching out with an Apple Business Manager account in hand, so this needs an actual outbound-ish
lead funnel (a landing page + the discovery questionnaire that's already drafted in the B2B brief),
which a two-person team may not have bandwidth to run persistently. Revenue is lumpy (per-deal, not
per-download) and the pipeline is currently zero committed customers, per the brief's own "open
questions."

**First shippable slice.** Publish `docs/kiosk/NumPad-for-Kiosks.md` as a public landing page with
a "Request a quote" form, route every inbound kiosk inquiry through the brief's existing discovery
questionnaire, and submit the first ABM Custom App record only once one lead clears the framework's
Product-A branch with a stated device count and budget.

## 3. Regional & Persona Bundles (a third price anchor)

**Pitch.** The pack-lineup proposal already fixes the *binary* pricing gap (pack-sum vs. Pro) with
6 packs + $8.99 Pro. The next lever is structural, not just corrective: today a buyer chooses
between "one pack" ($1.99) and "everything" ($8.99) with nothing in between, which is exactly the
two-point pricing anti-pattern behavioral-pricing research warns against (a middle anchor increases
average order value by giving the "I want two things, not six" buyer a real option instead of
forcing them to either under-buy or over-buy). Ship **persona bundles** that cut across the
catalog — a "Student Bundle" (Symbols & Science + Units & Conversion) or "Freelancer Bundle"
(Finance + Business & Invoicing, once #3 in the pack proposal ships) as their own non-consumable,
priced at a small discount to buying both separately but well under Pro. Pair with **deliberate
regional tiers**: rather than relying solely on Apple's "May Adjust Automatically" territory
pricing (which the pricing-packaging doc shows is already active), deliberately select a lower
explicit tier for India/Brazil/SEA on Pro specifically — those markets already show up in the
90-day territory mix (AMEA 10.2%, LatAm 6.0%) with zero deliberate PPP pricing applied yet.

**Wow moment.** A third price point closes exactly the gap the $8.99 fix leaves open: the buyer who
wants two things, not one and not six.

**Effort:** M — new non-consumable SKUs follow the exact `ProductCatalog`/`Monetization` gating
pattern already used for packs (a few new cases + entitlement-combination logic), no new
architecture; regional tiering is ASC-only.

**Risk.** SKU proliferation clutters the Store UI without a "Bundles" section to contain it; a
bundle priced too low cannibalizes Pro; at 5 IAPs/90 days pre-2.0, there is essentially no
statistical power to validate bundle attach rate rigorously — treat results as directional, not
conclusive, for several quarters.

**First shippable slice.** Ship exactly one bundle — Student Bundle (Symbols & Science + Units &
Conversion, once Units ships per the pack proposal) at $2.99 (a $1 discount vs. buying both) —
using the existing pack-wiring pattern verbatim, with a new "Bundles" row in the Store screen.

## 4. Try Before You Buy — In-Keyboard Pack Preview

**Pitch.** The baseline's sharpest number is 0.7% page conversion and "paying users sparse relative
to acquisition volume" — the funnel's weakest link isn't discovery, it's that a locked key shows a
lock chip and nothing else, so a curious user never *feels* what Programmer or Units is worth
before being asked to pay. Give every locked pack a strictly time-boxed, on-device free trial: the
first N taps (e.g., 15–20) on a locked key produce the **real** output — actual hex conversion,
actual unit math — via the engines that already exist and are fully built (`UnitConverter`,
`PackKeys`), tracked purely with a small `@UserDefault` counter (no network, no server, no new
StoreKit product). After the trial taps are spent, the existing lock-chip + deep-link-to-store
upsell (already wired end to end) takes over exactly as today. This reuses `isKeyLocked`, the
lock-chip overlay, and the `store_viewed` attribution pattern — the only new code is a trial-state
struct and one branch in the tap handler.

**Wow moment.** Users fall in love with a pack before they're ever asked to pay for it — using
nothing more than a tap counter already sitting next to the entitlement checks it augments.

**Effort:** S/M — one new `PackTrial` struct in `SharedExtensions.swift`, a small change to the
tap-handling path in `KeyboardViewController`, no new views.

**Risk.** Uninstall/reinstall resets the counter — there's no server-side proof of "already
trialed," so a determined user can loop the trial forever. Acceptable exposure for a $1.99 utility
(the abuse ceiling is low-value), but needs a controlled pilot and message-testing so the trial
reads as generous rather than as "the paywall doesn't work."

**First shippable slice.** Pilot on exactly one pack — Units & Conversion, once GA — with 15 free
conversions before the lock re-engages; compare attach-rate on that pack against the others for one
release cycle before deciding whether to extend the mechanic catalog-wide.

## 5. Custom Product Pages + Apple Search Ads Persona Funnel

**Pitch.** NumPad currently spends **$0 on acquisition** — 65K impressions and 3.32K product-page
views over 90 days are entirely organic (App Store Search + Browse per the baseline). The repo
already has draft Custom Product Pages (`marketing/aso-sales-audit/custom-product-pages-v2.md`,
`-v3.md`) and a working deep-link attribution field (`source=` on `numpad://store-preview`,
consumed by `StoreViewController` into `store_viewed`) — the missing piece is wiring 3–4 CPPs (one
each for Programmer, Finance/Business, Student/Units, Kiosk/iPad) to small, cheap Apple Search Ads
campaigns keyed to persona search terms ("hex keyboard," "unit converter keyboard," "tip calculator
keyboard," "kiosk number pad"), each campaign's landing CPP tagged so its resulting purchases are
attributable through infrastructure that **already ships today**. This is the first real
acquisition-side lever this app has pulled, ever — and per-persona ROAS is visible from day one
because nothing on the attribution side needs to be built.

**Wow moment.** The first paid acquisition channel this app has ever run, with per-persona ROAS
visible on day one because the attribution plumbing was already sitting there unused.

**Effort:** S — ASC/ASA configuration plus screenshot assembly reusing the existing generator
scripts in `marketing/`; zero app-side code changes.

**Risk.** Spending against a 0.7%-conversion page before the baseline doc's own recommended
screenshot/CPP fixes land risks burning a small budget on an unconverting page — sequence this
*after*, not instead of, that groundwork. Generic "numpad"/"keyboard" keywords are expensive and
low-intent; must stay disciplined on exact-match persona terms.

**First shippable slice.** Launch one CPP (Programmer persona) with a $5/day Apple Search Ads
campaign against 5 hand-picked exact-match keywords for two weeks, measured entirely through the
existing `source` attribution field — no new instrumentation required.
